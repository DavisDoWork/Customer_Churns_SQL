Customer Churn Analysis

I loaded a customer dataset into SQLite, cleaned it, and wrote SQL queries to find which customer groups churn most. Each question below shows the query, the result, and what I learned.

Table: customers · Tools: SQL, Python (pandas, sqlite3)

Questions

  • 1. What is the overall customer churn rate?
  • 2. How does churn vary by subscription type?
  • 3. Is there any correlation between age and customer churn?
  • 4. What is the typical tenure for churned customers?
  • 5. Is there any difference in churn rate between male and female customers?
  • 6. Is there any difference in churn rate and support calls?

  • Recommendations

Q1

What is the overall customer churn rate?

Query

SELECT
    COUNT(*) AS total_customers,
    SUM(CASE WHEN CustomerStatus = 'Churned' THEN 1 ELSE 0 END) AS churned_customers,
    ROUND(SUM(CASE WHEN CustomerStatus = 'Churned' THEN 1 ELSE 0 END) * 100.0 / COUNT(*), 2) AS ChurnRate
FROM customers;

Result

total_customerschurned_customersChurnRate (%)
643743049347.37

What I learned

Almost half of the customers in the dataset churned (47.37%). That is a high rate, so retention is a major business problem, not an edge case.

Q2

How does churn vary by subscription type?

Query

SELECT
    "Subscription Type",

Q4

What is the typical tenure for churned customers?

Query

SELECT
    TenureRange,
    COUNT(*) AS Total,
    SUM(CASE WHEN CustomerStatus = 'Churned' THEN 1 ELSE 0 END) AS ChurnedCustomers,
    ROUND(SUM(CASE WHEN CustomerStatus = 'Churned' THEN 1 ELSE 0 END) * 100.0 / COUNT(*), 2) AS ChurnRate
FROM customers
GROUP BY TenureRange
ORDER BY MIN(Tenure);

Result

TenureRangeTotalChurnedCustomersChurnRate (%)
1 year11360361631.83
2 year11287354631.42
3 year13764766455.68
4 year13968787756.39
5 year13995779055.66

What I learned

Customers with 1 or 2 years of tenure churn at about 31%. From year 3 onward the rate jumps to about 56%, and it stays there through year 5.

The jump happens between the second and third year, which suggests a point in the customer lifecycle where something changes, such as a contract renewal or a price change. I would check this before drawing a conclusion.

Q6

Is there any difference in churn rate and support calls?

Query

ALTER TABLE customers
ADD COLUMN SupportCallGroup VARCHAR(10);

UPDATE customers
SET SupportCallGroup = CASE
    WHEN "Support Calls" = 0 THEN '0'
    WHEN "Support Calls" BETWEEN 1 AND 3 THEN '1-3'
    WHEN "Support Calls" BETWEEN 4 AND 6 THEN '4-6'
    WHEN "Support Calls" BETWEEN 7 AND 9 THEN '7-9'
    ELSE '10+'
END;

SELECT
    SupportCallGroup,
    COUNT(*) AS Total,
    ROUND(SUM(CASE WHEN CustomerStatus = 'Churned' THEN 1 ELSE 0 END) * 100.0 / COUNT(*), 2) AS ChurnRate
FROM customers
GROUP BY SupportCallGroup
ORDER BY MIN("Support Calls");

Result

7-91991261.21
10+658760.70

What I learned

Churn goes up sharply once customers make more than three support calls. Customers with 0–3 calls churn at about 24%, and customers with 4 or more calls churn at 52–61%.

Support calls may be a warning sign. Frequent calls could mean unresolved problems, so they would make a useful early-warning signal for a retention team.

Summary

Insights

  1. Overall, 47.37% of customers churned.
  2. Churn is similar across subscription types: basic 48.28%, standard 47.33%, and premium 46.49%.
  3. Churn rises with age. Customers aged 18–44 churn at about 44–45%, 45–54 at 48.41%, and 55–65 at 52.72%.
  4. Customers with 1 or 2 years of tenure churn at about 31%, while customers with 3 to 5 years churn at about 56%. Churn rises between years 2 and 3 and stays high after that.
  5. Female customers churn at 55.05%, compared with 38.58% for male customers.
  6. Churn is much higher for customers who make 4 or more support calls (52–61%) than for those who make 0–3 calls (about 24%).

Recommendations

  1. Investigate why churn is highest among customers aged 45 and older (52.72% for ages 55–65), and what younger customers are looking for in the service.
  2. Investigate what happens between the second and third year of tenure, since churn roughly doubles after year 2.
  3. Treat customers with 4 or more support calls as an at-risk group and follow up on their unresolved issues.
  4. Look into what drives the gap between female (55.05%) and male (38.58%) churn before building targeted retention offers.
